The actual history

BEFORE THE
ALGORITHMS.

The point of putting this here isn't to rewrite 2007 with 2026 manners. It's to show the artifact, then look at it with older eyes.

2007

Seventeen years old. Learning by doing.

I was teaching myself this stuff by reading and experimenting before YouTube tutorials were the default. This became the first studio-quality rap song I knew of being recorded by someone at my high school.

2026

I remixed the recording instead of erasing it.

Kill You (Special 2026 version remix from 2007) is the old identity brought back into the present. Not a new threat. Not a cleaned-up autobiography. An old performance put under better lighting.

What we found in it

THE VIOLENCE
WASN'T THE WHOLE SONG.

When we pulled the lyrics apart, the most interesting thing was that the loudest material wasn't necessarily the deepest material.

01
Genre / persona

Horrorcore and shock rap, not a literal confession.

The repeated “I kill you” hook works like a chant of dominance and attention. The violence is theatrical: a persona trying to be impossible to ignore.

02
Underneath the flex

An adolescent power fantasy.

The character solves vulnerability with size, danger and control. That reads less like confidence than a teenager building armor out of performance.

03
Authority

Rebellion is aimed at more than one target.

The sanctuary / “house of God” material isn't just there for shock. It pushes against moral and religious authority — the same instinct that runs through the rest of the song: nobody gets to tell this character where the line is.

04
The meta line

“I'm just fucking with people's brains.”

That line basically admits the trick. The song knows it is provoking the listener. Shock isn't an accidental side effect; it is part of the performance.

05
The weirdly important detail

Then there's Tim Hortons.

A mundane Canadian setting sitting beside horror-movie aggression makes the persona feel even more obviously constructed. Everyday life and theatrical violence crash into each other.

06
The line underneath everything

“All of us strive to beat the demon inside / Don't let it override.”

That was the part that changed the reading. Buried inside all the threats is an admission that the real enemy can be internal. The song spends most of its time acting invulnerable, then briefly tells on itself.

Our read
The 17-year-old voice wanted to sound dangerous. The older reading is more interesting: power fantasy, rebellion, fear of vulnerability, performative masculinity — and a kid already writing about the fight with the thing inside himself.
