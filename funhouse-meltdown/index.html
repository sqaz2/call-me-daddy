This was not a blank prompt asking AI to invent everything. The rhythm and idea began in two recordings I made; the style prompt told Suno what musical world to build around them.

The source material

TWO VOICE CLIPS.
ONE STYLE PROMPT.

Clip one: me making the beat with my mouth—“unce, unce, unce.” It gave the experiment a human pulse before there was a finished instrumental.

Clip two: me trying to say all the problems in the world in a humorous, exaggerated rush. The growing list became part comedy and part overload.

I used those two recordings as the source material in Suno, then prompted the style of music I wanted. Suno expanded them into the full production, but the rhythm, voice and central idea began with me.

The humor is not that the problems are funny. It comes from the contrast: a simple dance pulse carrying a list so long that the overload becomes the message.

01 · Record the rhythm

Make the beat human.

The “unce” clip works like a vocal drum loop: repetition creates a pulse before instruments or production are added.

02 · Record the idea

Turn overload into performance.

The second clip takes a giant subject—the world’s problems—and delivers it as a fast, deliberately humorous stream.

03 · Prompt the style

Give Suno creative direction.

The style prompt tells Suno what musical character should surround the recordings. It shapes the production without replacing the source idea.

04 · Hear the transformation

Keep the voice inside the result.

The finished track is a conversation between the original human clips and the larger musical world generated around them.
