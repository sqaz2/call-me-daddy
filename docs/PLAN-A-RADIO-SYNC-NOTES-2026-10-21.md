## 2. Sync licensing: the presentation’s model

The introductory slide describes sync licensing as permission to use music with visual media in exchange for a licensing fee and royalties. It presents repeat earnings and exposure as possible benefits. Its Netflix example says a song earns an upfront payment and royalties “every time it streams”; that is the slide’s wording, not a verified payment rule or forecast. [S01]

The workflow taught is: [S05]

| Stage | What the presentation describes |
|---|---|
| Create | Record, mix, and master music to broadcast-ready standards. |
| Pitch | The artist or representative submits tracks to music supervisors or sync libraries. |
| Review | A supervisor assesses fit for a particular scene or project. |
| License | A license is signed and the fee negotiated. |
| Place | The music is used; the slide associates airing with royalties. |

The opportunity categories shown are television/streaming, film, commercials, video games, trailers, and digital content. Examples include dramas, documentaries, reality television, advertising campaigns, social-media spots, game menus and cutscenes, film/TV promos, YouTube, podcasts, TikTok brand content, and webisodes. Named services are examples on the slides, not evidence of an agreement with this program. [S02]

## 3. Readiness, deliverables, and pitching

The slides emphasize the following preparation requirements. These are the presentation’s standards, not a rights assessment of the Call Me Daddy catalog. [S01, S06, S07]

| Area | Guidance shown |
|---|---|
| Recording quality | Professional mix/master and clean, high-quality audio. |
| Rights | Own or control both the master and publishing; resolve samples and obtain co-writer paperwork. |
| Metadata | Make title, genre, BPM, key, mood, instrumentation, contact information, and rights information easy to find. These fields are combined across the slides. |
| Versions | Have stems and instrumental versions available; vocal-only/a cappella versions may also be requested. |
| Emotional fit | Match a specific mood, scene, or project rather than submitting solely because a song is good. |
| Pitch | Personalize the approach and explain project relevance. |

One slide labels six mistakes: poor mix quality, an unsuitable pitch approach, missing stems, uncleared samples, missing metadata, and working without a network. Its statements that generic pitches are automatically deleted, inadequate mixes are immediately rejected, or isolated artists remain invisible are promotional absolutes; these screenshots do not demonstrate universal policies. [S07]

The “Who Gets Placed?” slide argues that artists do not need a major-label deal or established fame, and that unknown artists can interest supervisors. It also says knowledge and connections can enable direct pitching without an expensive music lawyer. A later slide attributes missed opportunities to insufficient access, guidance, strategy, and community. These are the presenter’s framing, not proof that a paid academy is necessary. [S04, S09]

## 4. Songwriting guidance shown in a message attributed to Darnell Knight

A screen-shared message headed “Themes & Lyrics” recommends broadly usable emotional subjects. It groups naturally into: [S15]

- **Underdog and opportunity:** Cinderella stories, lucky breaks, sleepers, dark horses, survival, last chances, make-or-break efforts, avoiding danger, and a moment to shine.
- **Competition and perseverance:** Making history, legacy, being the star, feuding, fighting to the finish, winning streaks, bad luck, continuing the fight, being made for the challenge, and courage.
- **Human connection and development:** Hope, friendship, personal growth, social issues, celebration, teamwork, family, love, ambition, childhood, and inspiration.

The message says flexible or ambiguous lyrics can suit more situations, particularly in the hook. It recommends avoiding profanity in the hook and keeping the central theme consistent throughout the song.

Under “Tone and Delivery,” it stresses clear enunciation, especially in the hook. Its reasoning is that supervisors may want understandable lyrics to connect with a scene; unclear delivery could reduce vocal-placement options or favor instrumental use. This is contextual songwriting advice, not an instruction to change every existing song or remove the artist’s voice.

## 9. Optional Call Me Daddy preparation checklist

This checklist is derived from the presentation, not issued by Plan-A Radio or an identified buyer:

- [ ] Shortlist songs by emotional use and scene fit.
- [ ] Record title, version, BPM, key, mood, genre, instruments, contact, and rights status.
- [ ] List contributors, samples, production tools, and documents supporting any permissions claimed.
- [ ] Record the extent of AI involvement accurately; obtain the recipient’s applicable policy before asserting eligibility.
- [ ] Inventory full mixes, instrumentals, vocal-only versions, and stems; check that files match the intended song version.
- [ ] Review intelligibility, hook consistency, and technical quality against the actual brief.
- [ ] Confirm the authorized contact, exact deadline, deliverables, and submission method.
- [ ] Keep written terms and distinguish a submission, shortlist, signed license, and confirmed use.
