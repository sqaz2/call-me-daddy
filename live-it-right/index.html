A question disguised as a chorus

Who gets to
call it right?

We can become so afraid of living our lives wrong that we keep waiting for instructions. The waiting becomes our lives.

The idea began with a thought about overlooking what is already in front of us, then searching for someone wise enough to explain it. It became a hook about wanting somebody to tell us how to live.

We watch a talk about getting close to people, then leave someone’s message unanswered. We learn how to be creative, then wait to find out whether anyone will like what we make. There is always another explanation to find, another method to try, another person who seems qualified to tell us what comes next.

Someone else’s certainty.

Right according to our parents? The people who appear successful? A crowd of strangers deciding what deserves attention? Approval can become so familiar that we start asking other people which desires we should trust.

Sometimes we already recognize what matters. We miss someone. We want to make something. We know that a situation needs to change. Someone else saying it can feel like permission to believe ourselves.

Buying books on self-acceptance,
asking authors for corrections;

From the 16-bar verse · draft

Preparing can become a place to stay.

The rap verse follows that search as it gathers speed: lessons, self-assessments, borrowed ambitions, promises of certainty. Preparing feels productive. Waiting develops a vocabulary. Years can pass inside the process.

Living asks us to choose without a guarantee. A borrowed script can make that feel safer. If it falls apart, we have somebody to blame. It still cannot give the years back.

And then the song makes room for the person doing the asking.

03 / The bridge

Help me believe
that I can stand.

A line from the song in progress.

There’s a reason
we ask.

When decisions have hurt before, choosing again can be exhausting. Sometimes we want a hand to hold, somewhere to rest, somebody beside us when the outcome is uncertain.

The bridge asks for the confidence to stand when nothing goes to plan—and to keep believing there is something worth reaching for.

That is why the song says we. The voice asking these questions is caught in them too. It understands the comfort of being told what to do, and the hope that support might help us trust our own decisions.
