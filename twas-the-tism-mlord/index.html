THE CASTLE
OVERREACTS.

The narrator keeps having ordinary awkward, literal or overloaded moments. Medieval society keeps reaching for the most dramatic explanation available. The music acts like an exorcism is underway. The answer stays deadpan.

A normal moment.

A hint gets missed. Small talk stalls. Too many voices pile up. Something gets moved that absolutely did not need to be moved.

Obviously witchcraft.

Peasants, clergy, gargoyles, bells and the rest of the castle supply the completely unnecessary supernatural diagnosis.

Nay, m’lord.

The punchline is the calm correction. Then the sub-bass arrives and behaves considerably less calmly.

Making-of archive

THE VERSIONS
THAT LOST.

The final track was not one lucky generation. I made a pile of Suno cuts, narrowed the sound down, then kept editing the winner until the ending became its own little dubstep malfunction.

01

The meme seed

A crowned medieval frog and one line: “’Twas the tism, m’lord.” That was enough to establish the entire universe.

02

Suno cutting room

Multiple generations and cuts tested different ways to carry the same joke. Most of them were useful for one thing, not the whole song.

03

The sound survives

The chosen direction kept the intimate dark R&B vocal, cathedral weight and heavy half-time dubstep instead of turning into a generic novelty song.

04

Surgical stutters

Weak stretches were removed rather than tolerated. Tiny repeated fragments bridge the cuts so the edits become part of the rhythm.

05

Full tweak-out

The outro stops pretending to be stable. Stutters spread out, then the last section continuously fractures until the track is gone.
